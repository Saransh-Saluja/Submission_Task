0 · First principles: what is actually true?

I started by deleting the idea of a “recipe page” and asking what is physically true about a person cooking. Every design decision below is derived from one of these five facts, not borrowed from a pattern library.

Fundamental truthWhat followsDesign decision
1. Capacitive screens need a clean, dry fingertip. Flour, oil and water break this, and dirty glass is also a hygiene problem.The fingertip is the wrong input. Free body parts are the forearm, wrist, voice and eyes.Voice and air gestures first; elbow-sized targets as the reliable fallback.
2. Eyes belong to the food. A cook’s glance at the screen lasts about a second, from 3–5 ft.Legibility is an angle, not a point size. Each glance can carry one idea.Size type by arcminutes at 4 ft; one verb per step, ≤7 words; everything else hidden until asked.
3. The kitchen attacks every channel, each in a different way: noise kills voice, steam kills camera, hands in the way kill touch.No single input can be reliable. Independent channels fail independently.A fallback ladder, where every failure state names the next working channel on screen.
4. Cooking is a race against time. Only timers have hard deadlines, and missing one burns food.Timers are the one element allowed to interrupt, and they must be identified by what is cooking.Auto-start, name by food, soonest-first sizing, full-screen takeover on ring.
5. Any sensing system, voice and gesture especially, will sometimes be wrong.Reliability comes from cheap recovery, not perfect recognition.300 ms hold filter, 5 s undo on every hands-free action, visible “what I heard” caption.

Sizing the elbow target from first principles: a fingertip target needs roughly 9–10 mm. A forearm or elbow contact patch is several times bigger and far less precise, so I assume (estimate, to validate in testing) a ~35 mm minimum. On an 11″ tablet that gives Next at about 2.1″ × 1.4″ and Back at about 1.3″ × 1.4″, placed on the lowest edge where the arm already rests.

Why this matters: a standard recipe page assumes seated reading, clean hands and full attention. Remove all three and none of its layout survives, which is why this isn’t a restyled recipe page.

1 · Assumptions

Device

11″ landscape tablet propped on a counter. Fixed, so it can use the front camera and mic continuously.

Distance

Cook stands 3–5 ft away; design for the far end, 4 ft. Arm reach to screen is not guaranteed.

Kitchen

Noisy (chimney fan 60–70 dB), steamy, glare from a window or tube light, one or two pans going.

User

Home cook, often mid-task with wet or floury hands, switching between Hindi/English phrases. Cooks one recipe at a time but runs 2–4 timers.

2 · Three principles

① One thing, one verb

Main view shows only the current step, ≤7 words, led by a verb. Why: a glance is ~1 s; a verb is the one word that tells you what your hands should do.

② Never one input

Voice → gesture → elbow target. Each failure state names the next fallback on screen. Why: fans, steam and gloves each break a different channel; none breaks all.

③ Cheap to undo

Every hands-free action gets a 5 s undo. Why: hands-free input will misfire; recoverable errors beat perfect recognition.

4 · Non-touch interaction ladder

🎙 Voice (primary)

“Chef, next / back / double it / start timer”. Pill turns amber while listening and prints what it heard, so users trust it. Hindi/English aliases (“agla”).

✋ Air gesture

Open palm = pause timers; horizontal swipe = next/back. A ring shows the hand is detected before the action fires.

💪 Elbow hold

300 ms hold with a fill ring. Short enough to feel instant, long enough to ignore a graze. 5 s undo covers the rest.

👁 Proximity

Walk away: screen dims, timers stay large. Return: full brightness, step re-announced in the voice caption.

5 · Hard constraints, solved

Many timers

Auto-start on entering a step (with undo). Max 1 large + the rest compact. On ring: full-screen takeover, pulse + sound + two giant buttons (+1 min / Done). Two rings: stacked, each independent.Why: naming by food avoids “which Timer 2?”; takeover prevents burnt garlic.

Scaling

×1 ×2 ×3 at top of rail, or “Chef, double it”. Changed numbers flash amber for 1 s. Timers do not change, and the interface says so when scale changes cooking time.Why: instant visible feedback, with no modal and no keyboard.

Cognitive load

Progressive disclosure: only this step’s ingredients, steps ≤7 words, detail on voice “Chef, details”.

Signal over noise: the glance model

1 second

Verb in amber. Tells the hands what to do.

3 seconds

The one-line step and this step’s ingredients with amounts.

10 seconds

Timers and progress dots: how long, and how far through.

Deleted from a normal recipe page: hero photo, author story, ratings and reviews, nutrition, comments, ads, the full ingredient list and every future step. All still exist; they are one voice command away (“Chef, details”).

Why: every element on screen must answer “will this change what my hands do in the next 10 seconds?” If not, it is noise.

Sound language: soft tick = confirmed; double beep = timer. Reduced-motion users get the same states with no animation.

Details I sweated

BACK is dimmed on step 1; NEXT becomes DONE ✓ on the last step and ends with “Enjoy”.
Scaling mid-cook only changes remaining steps; used ingredients stay ticked.
“+1 min” can repeat; an alarm never auto-dismisses.
Screen never sleeps while a timer runs; locks 10 min after the last one.
Setting to mirror the elbow targets for left-handed cooks.
No colour-only signals: every state also has a word or icon.
Hindi/English voice aliases (“agla”, “peechhe”).
Voice commands run on-device, so they work with no Wi-Fi.
Camera indicator light and a one-tap off for privacy.

7 · Type, colour, and the 4-ft maths

Legibility is an angle, not a point size: height = 2 × distance × tan(angle ÷ 2). A common HF standard is ≥16 arcmin for text; at 48 in that is 0.22 in cap height. We then aim higher for the things that matter.

ElementSizeCap heightAngle @ 4 ft
Verb64 pt0.62 in≈ 44′
Step line36 pt0.35 in≈ 25′
Ingredients28 pt0.27 in≈ 19′
Minimum anywhere24 pt0.23 in≈ 17′ ✓

Colour: off-white #F3EEE6 on charcoal #121110, not pure white on black, to cut glare and halation. One accent (amber #FFB347) means “act now”; red = urgent only; green = done. Colour is never the sole signal: every state also has a word or icon. Heavy weight (800–900) on the verb survives steam and window glare. Fixed positions: NEXT never moves, so muscle memory forms by the 2nd step.

Feedback without haptics: sound (soft tick on action, loud alarm), colour pulse, and motion (step slides left). Respects reduced-motion.

8 · Success metrics (how I’d test)

Step advance with wet hands ≤ 2 s, ≥ 95% success
False-trigger undo rate < 5% of advances
Zero missed timers across a 30-min cook (diary + log)
Legibility check: read verb at 5 ft in glare, 10/10 users
